# Glass Buoy first redesign: GO on major forms, REVISE on the glass

The enclosure, floats, rim, pylons, cargo and empty-ocean composition are a GO and need no remodel. The glass still reads as a painted sports court, not glass, and Night has no light of its own. That glass fix is bounded material work, but it blocks coloured approval because glass is this stage's whole identity.

## What reads at battle scale

- **Floating enclosure: works.** The white pontoon ring on four drum floats sits on real water with a contact reflection. The on/above/under ambiguity is gone, as are the yellow band, the coral pads and the domes. It is still a little frame-like, because the floats only peek out as small ears at the corners.
- **Glass: fails as glass.** It is a flat mid-value indigo. The white inner border and the thin white cross through the centre read as court markings. The sheen bands are barely visible, and only near the far edge.
- **Under-glass depth: not visible.** I can see no wreck, manta, fish or seabed in any of the four frames, the oblique included.
- **Rim versus glass: reads by colour only.** The dark navy band against violet is clear enough to play. The rim has no visible tread at this scale, so it looks like a painted running track rather than a grippy surface.
- **Pylons: good.** The fat orange cylinders with white caps and dark collars are chunky and toy-like, and the four-obstacle layout is intact. They do not yet read as beacons, because no lamp is visible by day or at Night.
- **Yellow cargo: reads well.** The X-braced crates are the strongest colour accent. They are close to dominating at this density, but acceptable.
- **Empty ocean: right idea.** One small buoy on the left is the correct restraint. The sea is a flat, dull blue close in hue and value to the glass, so the white frame alone separates the two.
- **Eight players and HUD: clear.** The far rail sits below the HUD. All suits read except the purple one, which is low contrast where the violet glass meets the navy rim.
- **Low: keeps the identity.** It is nearly identical to High.
- **Night: weak.** Everything turns one blue-grey and the crates go olive. There is no warm accent, which was one of my acceptance criteria.

## Required next step (bounded, materials only)

**1. Make the glass read as glass.**
- **Value:** darken to deep ultramarine-violet, roughly `#2a2170` at the centre to `#3d35a0` at the edges. The current tone is about a third too light. This also fixes the purple player's contrast and separates the glass from the sea.
- **Lines:** replace the white cross and white border with dark gunmetal mullions about 0.12 cell wide, flush with the surface. Use a 3×3 pane layout, not a centre cross.
- **Edge:** give the glass one bright bevelled lip about 0.15 cell wide where it meets the rim.
- **Sheen:** two or three broad diagonal bands, 1.5 to 2.5 cells wide, 12 to 18% lighter, soft-edged, drifting slowly across the whole court.
- **Reflections:** soft mirrored reflections of pylons and crates in the surface at about 25% strength on Medium and High. This is the strongest cheap cue for polished glass. Low keeps the bands only.
- **Depth:** whatever sits below must be large, soft-edged and desaturated, and overlapped by crisp mullions and crisp object shadows on top. That contrast is what creates depth with a fixed camera.

**2. Give Night its own light.**
- Add a warm lamp on each pylon cap with a small glow pool on the glass.
- Light the buoy.

## Recommended finishing (not blocking)

- **Corners and floats:** chamfer the frame corners by about one cell and enlarge the floats about 1.4×, so they protrude a full cell. That weakens the picture-frame reading.
- **Tread:** add rows of pale round studs about 0.25 cell on a slightly lighter navy, kept matte against the glossy glass.
- **Sea:** push it a little greener or lighter than the glass.
