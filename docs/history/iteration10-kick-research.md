# Iteration ten: kicking, impact and bomb timing

Verified 2026-10-20. Sources are Nintendo's original manual and direct inspection of cached original gameplay, rather than recollections or fan summaries. Numerical rules proposed below are project tuning.

## What the publisher establishes

The [original English manual](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/downloads/games_8/emanuals/nintendo_8/Manual_Nintendo64_Bomberman64_EN.pdf), printed pp. 4–5 (PDF page 4), explicitly describes two kick routes: run into a set bomb, or stand still and press A to place a bomb and press A again to kick it. R stops a bomb after kicking. Thus a planted bomb is a movable physical object, and a kick initiates motion without requiring pickup. The English booklet supplies the run-into-bomb detail that the shorter [French controller spread](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/downloads/games_8/emanuals/nintendo_8/Manual_Nintendo64_Bomberman64_FR.pdf) does not spell out.

The same English spread assigns B to lifting a planted bomb or paralyzed enemy and to throwing the held object in the facing direction. Holding B and pressing A creates a held bomb. Repeated A while carrying creates a pumped bomb. The control stick can alternatively throw depending on the Options setting. The booklet therefore distinguishes ground kick from a carried throw; a generic projectile/shoot button is an adaptation.

The item spread, printed pp. 26–27 (PDF page 15), gives normal bomb power up to level three and pumped bomb power up to level six. Remote bombs detonate with Z. The manual does not describe wall impact starting an automatic fuse; preserving command detonation after a wall stop is our inference from that rule, not an explicit statement about wall collisions. Its Skull entry includes faster-than-usual explosion as a curse. The manual establishes a distinction between ordinary delayed explosions and remote triggering, but supplies no duration in seconds.

## Implementation policy and limits

For this project, a kick should have a visible start and directional travel, and collision with a player should produce an immediately legible impact reaction. A short upward arc, horizontal impulse, temporary control interruption and landing/recovery state are reasonable adaptations to the requested knockup behavior. Their height, speed, duration and immunity window are tuning choices; the inspected sources do not establish those constants or prove that specific reaction for every kicked bomb.

Use explicit fuse policy for each bomb mode. A normal bomb should have a finite timed fuse; a pumped bomb should retain a predictable finite timed fuse unless it is remote; a remote bomb should await its owner's detonation action. Whether holding pauses that finite timer, whether a pump refreshes it, any minimum post-release window, and whether remote bombs have a safety timeout all require deliberate project rules. The publisher evidence does not justify describing any chosen value or reset behavior as faithful reverse engineering.

A practical test matrix should distinguish normal ground kick, pumped ground kick, ordinary throw and remote throw/kick; verify player contact before any blast; and separately verify wall contact, carried timing, release timing and remote detonation. Record the selected project constants with the implementation so gameplay tests assert intentional policy.

## Chosen constants and diagnosis

The deterministic `BombImpactChecks` loop reproduced two missing reactions before the change: a kicked bomb had no launch hop, and its victim was stunned in place with no upward/horizontal impulse. The selected shot travels at 12 cells/second (player maximum 4.5), with a .28-second/.18-cell visible hop. Authoritative collision still sweeps the entire .35-cell bomb radius in increments no longer than .1 cells; the hop does not grant permission to cross stone. Player impact applies 4.2 cells/second horizontally and 4.5 upward through the shared authoritative player impulse helper. The outgoing-tail guard continues to protect a kicker following their own shot. Billiard contact transfers the shot without refreshing either fuse.

The initial finite normal/pumped fuse checks passed before this change; the normal bomb also reached stone. The pumped case was subsequently tightened to require an actual kicked stop against the wall, because a short thrown bomb could instead hit its thrower and stop before reaching it. This finding does not invalidate the reported stuck-bomb experience: a separate three-player callback fixture reproduced an owner's remote remaining armed indefinitely after departure. A two-player fixture initially concealed that bug because the round reset removed all bombs after departure. The regression therefore keeps two other players alive, which isolates bomb lifecycle from round cleanup.

Normal bombs retain their 2.5-second fuse, paused during carry and resumed after release; pumping and kicking do not reset it. A reachable, alive owner with the remote ability keeps command detonation. The remote command now also works during roll/flight, without requiring the bomb to reach a wall first; carried bombs remain excluded. A remote whose owner is missing, dead, a ghost, disconnected or unable to send the remote command converts to its remaining ordinary fuse and emits a `remote-fuse` presentation event. This is an explicit project cleanup rule, not a claim about the original cartridge.

## Real-scene verification

`python3 tools/playtest/run.py --scenario kick-physics --output artifacts/iteration10-kick --timeout 180` passed 64 assertions with eight actual windowed captures and zero runtime errors in `artifacts/iteration10-kick/20261020T204417.775531Z-kick-physics/`. The scenario uses three ordinary human practice seats on the stock Sunken Temple, real movement to the training remote cache, contact kick, victim flight/landing, remote waiting and command, and an ordinary diagonal bomb kicked into the outside corner of the raised terrace at (5,11). No fixture stage or state writes are used. Its fuse ends in a rendered explosion while the kicker retreats safely.

The first passing visual run exposed two evidence weaknesses during inspection: the hop capture was late enough to show contact, and the corner witness's route collected two actual fire upgrades, making a short retreat insufficient. The final scenario captures the shot sooner, places the victim farther away, and verifies safe retreat beyond the upgraded blast. The small hop remains subtle at the full-map camera scale; the impulse and prone flight reaction are clearer cues.

The final v0.10.0 standalone full suite, `artifacts/iteration10-final/20261020T225239.321415Z-all`, includes the kick, body-contact and revised grapple scenarios: 1,470 assertions, 109 unique captures, no runtime errors or capture fallbacks. The kick presentation now adds a short anticipation/leg follow-through, counter-arm/torso motion, small speed streaks and a stronger original synthesized cue. These are original project assets and adaptation choices.
