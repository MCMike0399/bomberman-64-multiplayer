# Art direction review: Blast Circuit stage expansion

**Verdict:** none of the four new stages meets the bar yet, and the user's "cheap, lacks style" reaction is accurate. Top Rules and Up & Down are harmless but too small to change anyone's impression. The fixes needed are rebuilt forms and value structure, not more props or colour tweaks.

## 1. The actual identity, and why the candidates read cheap

The shipped stages that work (Sunken Temple, Up & Down, Greedy Trap) share a grammar:

- **Massing, not outline.** The arena is a sculptural object with vertical drama: the temple's stepped pyramid, Up & Down's piers on fluted drums. You could recognise each from a grey thumbnail.
- **Three-value structure.** Sunken Temple has a dark cobbled court, warm light sandstone and saturated turquoise sea. Play space always separates from surroundings by value and hue.
- **One hero material with authored trim.** Examples are the meander frieze bands, the dark bedrock plinth at the waterline, burgundy brick with dark caps, and purple panels against pale stone.
- **Toy-scale chunkiness.** Details are sized for a 10 cm figurine: fat bevels, thick caps, round barrels. Nothing is hairline.
- **Surroundings composed as vignettes.** The lighthouse on its rock with a jetty and the ruined arch with fallen drums each tell one small story, placed asymmetrically, with open water as rest space.
- **Premium water** as the saturated, living negative space.

The established set is not uniformly good. Circuit Garden is flat slabs, Rock Garden's enclosure is a plain grey box wall, and Top Rules' court is white cubes on a checker. Benchmark against Sunken Temple and Up & Down, not the average.

Problems common to the candidates, as observed:

1. **Tray construction.** All four are a rectangle with a constant-width band around it, with no massing, plinth or profile. Field of Grass is a rug in a frame, Lost at Sea a picture frame, Sea Sick a box lid, Gutter a tank.
2. **Kit reuse reads as reskin.** Field of Grass uses Rock Garden's boulders, windmill, trees and flower beds. Gutter uses Greedy Trap's brick, block cubes, blue-roof houses and waterwheels. They look like variants, not places.
3. **No value hierarchy.** Field is green on green on green. Lost at Sea is mid-value grey-teal everywhere. Gutter is blue water against blue-grey paving against a flat periwinkle void.
4. **Hairline detail.** The Field fence, Sea Sick rope rails, the roughly 30 thin bars per Gutter grate and the Gutter flow streaks all read as noise or scanlines at battle camera, the opposite of chunky toy scale.
5. **Scattered, symmetric dressing.** Trees and flower beds alternate evenly around Field, lamps and shrubs line Gutter, and four coral pads sit at Lost at Sea's corners. Each is an even scatter with no single focal vignette.
6. **Hero props hidden under the HUD.** Gutter's houses and wheels, Sea Sick's yard and sail, Lost at Sea's ring object and Field's cottage are all cropped by the HUD.
7. **Water stages without the premium water.** Gutter's water is an opaque saturated blue sheet. Lost at Sea has no readable water surface at all.
8. **Uniform tiling.** Sea Sick's deck is square parquet, not planking, and Field's bank tops are flat green with a faint grid.

## 2. Stage-by-stage judgement

### Field of Grass: not acceptable
- **What's wrong:** there is no valley. The reference's identity is two craggy brown walls making a V around a green floor. Here the banks are flat lawn terraces, a dark board-like face and a tan plank of slope. The grass ends in a ruler-straight edge overhanging a tan floor, like a doormat. The boulders are all one size and one grey.
- **Not an art problem:** grass hiding bodies and bombs is intended. The problem is the container, not the concealment.
- **Keep:** the blade density and green gradient, which are a decent start, and the bank spawns.
- **Remove:** the hairline fence, tan tray floor, Rock Garden boulders, cloned meadow exterior and the crate prop colliding with the "Local battle" label.
- **Redesign:** banks, grass boundary, cover objects, exterior.

### In the Gutter: closest structurally, generic in execution
- **What's wrong:** the posts are plain brick boxes with slab caps. The centre marker is a box with a yellow lid, where the reference has a distinct ornament. The grates are thin fencing with no machinery, though they are the stage's hazard and should be its stars. The flow streaks read as scanlines. The exterior is a large blank grey quay with a void behind it.
- **Diagnostic artefact:** the reflection diagnostic makes cubes look double-stacked, so I am not judging reflections.
- **Keep:** topology, burgundy and cream post colours, start-on-posts.
- **Remove:** flow streak lines, cloned houses and wheels, hairline bars.
- **Redesign:** water, grates, centre marker, post profile, upstream and downstream ends.

### Sea Sick: right concept, wrong object
- **Agree with the rejection.** Portholes on this tray will not fix it. A ship is defined by curvature (sheer line, tumblehome, bow taper) and by an axis from bow to stern. The candidate has neither.
- **Other faults:** the mast is a square post, the two posts are boxes with pyramid caps, and the hull sides are flat brown noise.
- **Keep:** the barrels (round, hooped, chunky, the best new asset in the set), the teal trim idea, the stairs and raised side decks.
- **Redesign:** hull, deck planking, mast and posts, sea state.

### Lost at Sea: weakest, restart the presentation
- **What's wrong:** the "glass" reads as a dark hole, with no sheen, panes, edge thickness or reflection. Crate shadows fall onto something below, which reinforces the pit reading. The sand-yellow outer band reads as cardboard. The pylons are orange boxes with a slot.
- **Surroundings:** a flat matte plane with octagonal pads and glossy domes that look like placeholders. It is unclear whether the platform is on, above or under the sea. The palette is the most desaturated in the game.
- **Keep:** the white riveted rim idea, warm pylons, X crates, fish as a notion.
- **Remove:** yellow band, coral pads, domes, matte ground.
- **Redesign:** everything visual.

### Top Rules refinement: marginal improvement, not worth more iterations
- **Change observed:** the softer banding is better than the old candy stripes, but the ring is now a flat opaque washer and the blue world reads as marbled noise.
- **Why it doesn't matter much:** both planets sit cropped at the frame edges, so the pass barely changes the impression.
- **Real weaknesses, untouched:** a pure black void, white cumulus puffs floating in space, corner turrets cut by the HUD, and plain white cubes on the court.
- **Recommendation:** accept this pass as "no worse", and stop polishing planets.

### Up & Down refinement: fine, minor
- **Change observed:** the stratified stacks with grass caps are better than the smooth tapered cylinders.
- **Risk:** check the fine horizontal strata for shimmer on Low. Use fewer, thicker bands if in doubt.
- **Recommendation:** approve. This stage was already one of the best.

## 3. Art-direction concepts

One constraint drives all four: Low hides scenery, so identity must live in the arena body itself.

### Field of Grass: "Cutaway Hollow"
- **Silhouette:** a U-shaped gulch presented as a slice of earth lifted out of the countryside. The near face is a diorama cross-section showing soil strata, buried pebbles, roots and one burrow.
- **Palette:** warm red-ochre banks, deep emerald grass with wheat-gold tips, cool muted exterior. The gold tips separate it from Rock Garden's lawn.
- **Signature forms:**
  1. The strata cut face.
  2. Rounded, slumped loam banks with chunky retaining stones and overhanging turf lips.
  3. Mossy standing stones in three sizes with lichen caps, replacing the boulders.
- **Grass:** fewer, wider, curved blades in clumps, dark at the root and light at the tip. The boundary should be ragged and climb the bank foot.
- **Story:** one asymmetric vignette on a bank shoulder, such as a leaning stile with a hay rick. Close the far end with a dry-stone wall and leave the front open.
- **Life:** travelling wind-gust bands, blades parting around helmets, two butterflies.
- **Procedural route:** extrude the bank from a profile curve with low-frequency displacement, and paint strata as world-Y shader bands. This needs no texture work.

### In the Gutter: "Copper Lock"
- **Silhouette:** two tall sluice-gate machines flank the court. Each grate becomes a portcullis of five to seven fat bars with rounded finials, hung from a verdigris gantry with one big gear and a counterweight. An arched inflow culvert sits at one end and a stepped weir at the other.
- **Palette:** glazed burgundy brick, cream stone, verdigris and brass, jade-clear shallows over a patterned mosaic floor.
- **Signature forms:**
  1. The gate machines.
  2. An original brass weathervane-fish on a striped pole at the centre, swinging to the current.
  3. Bollard-like posts with rounded caps, mooring rings and an algae tide line.
- **Water:** use the existing clear-shallows shader. Show current with foam wakes trailing downstream of every post and block, plus a few drifting leaves. This reads direction better than streaks and looks premium.
- **Life:** the gear ticks when the direction changes, and lanterns give warm reflections at Night.

### Sea Sick: "The Tubby Cutter"
- **Axis:** run the ship left to right, so the existing raised side decks become sterncastle (left) and forecastle (right). Bow and stern then extend into the unused widescreen margins instead of under the HUD.
- **Silhouette:** a fat toy hull whose near side bulges below the deck as a curved belly. Add a sheer line rising to both ends, a cream wale stripe, a stubby bowsprit and a stern lantern.
- **Palette:** honey deck, deep navy hull, cream and red trim, brass, with a darker and choppier sea than the lagoon stages.
- **Signature forms:**
  1. The curved hull with rudder and stern windows that glow at Night.
  2. A round tapered mast with a chunky band, pin rail and thick shrouds landing outside play.
  3. The two posts rebuilt as a capstan and bitts.
- **Deck:** long planks along the axis with staggered butt joints and peg dots.
- **Life:** the sail is off-frame, so let its shadow sweep the deck with the roll. Add bow foam, a swinging lantern and whitecaps.
- **Procedural route:** loft hull cross-sections along a spline. It replaces the box below the deck line only, so topology is untouched.

### Lost at Sea: "The Glass Buoy"
- **Decision:** make it a surface platform floating alone in open ocean, with a glass floor looking down into the deep. This resolves the on/above/under ambiguity.
- **Silhouette:** a chamfered white-enamel pontoon ring on four fat corner floats, riding real premium water with a foam contact line.
- **Palette:** ultramarine-violet glass (distinct from Blizzard's cyan), white enamel, black rubber tread, signal-orange pylons, yellow crates.
- **Signature forms:**
  1. Glass that reads as glass: a bright bevelled rim, a few large panes with steel mullions flush with the surface, broad diagonal sheen bands as in the reference, and soft pylon reflections.
  2. A lit world below at real depth, with a sunken wreck and one large slow shadow (a manta or whale) crossing every minute or so.
  3. Pylons as beacon towers with blinking lamps.
- **Rim:** tread plate with big rivets, so nonslip versus slippery is visible in the material.
- **Story:** one distant bell buoy and a drifting raft. Emptiness is the point of the name.
- **Low:** bake the depth gradient and sheen into the glass shader so identity survives without scenery.

## 4. Implementation order by visual payoff

**Shared rules (cheap, do first):**
- No line thinner than about 3 px at 1280×800 battle camera.
- No unmodified prop reused from another stage.
- Every hero prop sits outside the HUD band.
- One focal vignette per stage, placed asymmetrically.

**Order:**
1. **Lost at Sea.** Furthest from the bar. Do a shape study first.
2. **Sea Sick hull.** Do a shape study first: a grey untextured hull from battle and oblique cameras, approved before any materials.
3. **Field of Grass.** Banks and cut face, then grass boundary, then stones. A shape study of the bank profile is advisable.
4. **In the Gutter.** Water and wakes first, then gate machines, then the centre vane.
5. **Up & Down.** Merge after the Low shimmer check.
6. **Top Rules.** Freeze. If time remains, soften the ring and remove the clouds.

**Acceptance criteria, for every stage:**
- **Thumbnail:** at 320 px wide in greyscale, the stage is identifiable by silhouette, and play area, structure and surroundings sit in three distinct values.
- **Low:** with scenery hidden, the stage is still recognisably itself, with no shimmer or moiré.
- **High:** adds richness, not new clutter.
- **Night:** players, bombs and edges stay readable, with at least one warm light accent.
- **Readability:** helmets, aim markers and hazard cues are never occluded by decoration.

**Reject on sight:**
- **Field of Grass:** any straight grass edge, or banks that read as flat planes.
- **In the Gutter:** any streak lines, or opaque water.
- **Sea Sick:** any straight hull side visible from the battle camera.
- **Lost at Sea:** glass that reads as a hole, or a matte ground plane.
