# Iteration eleven: kick weight, travel and stopping

Inspected 2026-10-20. This note supplements [iteration ten](iteration10-kick-research.md) with tighter inspection of actual 1997 Bomberman 64 gameplay. Original footage and contact sheets remain ignored under `artifacts/research/iteration11/`; they are research references, not distributable game assets. None of the observations below establishes an original simulation constant.

## Publisher evidence: kick and throw are different actions

The [Nintendo-hosted English instruction booklet](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/downloads/games_8/emanuals/nintendo_8/Manual_Nintendo64_Bomberman64_EN.pdf), printed pp. 4–5 / PDF page 4, establishes a ground kick by running into a planted bomb, or by standing still, planting with A and pressing A again. R stops a previously kicked bomb. Its separate throw instructions use B to lift and throw an object. Stick displacement, or how long B is pressed in the alternate control setting, determines **throw** distance. That sentence does not establish charged kicks, different kick strengths, or a fixed kick range. The manual supplies no wind-up, recovery, kick speed, travel distance, deceleration or action-lock duration.

The existence of the R brake supports a sustained moving-bomb state that the player can deliberately end. It does not by itself prove infinite travel, zero friction, or every collision's behavior.

### Follow-up: contact, terrain and longer travel

- [Same multiplayer recording, 00:14.30–00:17.20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPjnYY_Gomo&t=14s), inspected every 0.1 second (`kick-contact-after.jpg`): after the red bomb meets black around 14.70, the bomb moves a short way back up/left, opposite its incoming direction, then is stationary by roughly 15.30 through at least 17.20. Black was already prone before contact. This encounter supports a collision that interrupts forward travel and produces a short recoil; it does not support passing freely through the victim. Because the player is near the platform edge and other bombs, this is not proof of an invariant player-only collision rule.
- [Same recording, 01:41.80–01:42.40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPjnYY_Gomo&t=101s), inspected every 0.1 second (`platform-leadin.jpg`): black stands over a small bomb on the lower-left side of the central tiled mound, then the bomb moves down the tiles onto grass. There is no compulsory stop at the visible tile/grass transition.
- [Same recording, 01:42.50–01:45.40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPjnYY_Gomo&t=102s), inspected every 0.1 second (`platform-leadin.jpg`, `kick-over-mound.jpg`): white has a small bomb at foot level at the lower-left mound foot and makes a short kick around 102.70. The bomb travels diagonally northeast across the tiled mound, leaves the right edge around 103.50–103.60, continues over the upper-right grass, and reaches the outer upper-right perimeter around 104.60–104.80. It then changes course toward the left along the upper boundary around 104.90–105.40. This is about two seconds of visible travel before the perimeter encounter and covers a substantial diagonal span of the arena. It decisively contradicts interpreting the 3-second short stop as the original's compulsory short kick range. The low object/shadow relationship, foot-level origin and continuous route across the mound distinguish this from an overhead throw.

The mound witnesses support crossing the original arena's visible shallow raised/tiled transitions in both directions. They do **not** establish that bombs may cross every abrupt vertical ledge, the original world-height profile, slope limits, or a safe numerical step threshold for our terrain. The perimeter witness supports a glancing/deflecting interaction in that encounter; it does not establish that every direct wall impact reflects. Increasing speed is not a remedy for an early stop caused by terrain rejection.

## Follow-up: the victim reaction is small

The user's requested reduction in bomb knockout is supported by a clearer upright-victim event: [XFactor1111 multiplayer, 01:43.50–01:45.05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPjnYY_Gomo&t=103s), inspected every 0.05 second in `white-contact-103.jpg`. Black has a small dark bomb at foot level on the left side of the central mound, makes a kick around 103.65–103.75, and the bomb reaches upright white around 103.85–104.00. Stars appear immediately. White moves only roughly **one to two standing character widths** from the contact neighborhood and settles close by around 104.30–104.40. This is a screen-space comparison against the character in the same shot, not a conversion to world cells. The pose briefly rocks/pops relative to the shadow; the sequence does not justify a large aerial launch or a numerical original hop height.

White remains visibly dizzy/starred through at least 105.05. Subsequent other-player involvement complicates the actual return to normal play, and the original [manual's controller spread](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/downloads/games_8/emanuals/nintendo_8/Manual_Nintendo64_Bomberman64_EN.pdf), printed pp. 4–5, explicitly permits faster recovery by rotating the stick while paralyzed. Controller inputs are hidden. Thus the roughly 0.4–0.5-second interval from contact to nearby settling is an observation about this reaction's visible motion, **not** an exact total knockout duration or a proof of its unassisted recovery timer.

A restrained impulse with a brief readable interruption, close landing and separate recovery is consistent with this witness. Reducing the project's horizontal impact speed from 4.2 to 2.2 and vertical speed from 4.5 to 2.6, while retaining the chosen landing recovery, is a deliberate tuning proposal. Those constants are not measured from the original. Judge the resulting footage by nearby displacement and quick settling relative to body size; an impressive long launch would miss the requested small stumble.

## Consequences for this project

The most defensible reference is a planted bomb receiving a short, readable kick and continuing as a low moving object. The 14-second sequence has enough travel time for the viewer to follow it and anticipate contact. It does not resemble an instantaneous shot or the overhead arc of a carried throw. A pronounced lob, new minimum range, strong automatic friction, long charge, or enforced recovery delay would be deliberate project adaptations, not findings from these references.

For iteration eleven, use the reference to evaluate whether the foot connects before travel begins, whether the torso/leg follow-through reads at the normal camera scale, and whether a viewer can track a bomb across several body widths before impact. If the action needs more weight, adjust original presentation and tune travel deliberately; do not label any chosen wind-up, recovery or cells-per-second value as reverse-engineered. Keep the kick stop policy explicit and independently verify player collision, wall collision, smooth terrain transitions and commanded stopping where supported. Separate speed from available travel distance: a terrain collision can truncate travel even with a large speed/fuse budget.

Still unestablished: exact original anticipation/recovery duration; whether the original blocks movement during either phase; ground speed in world units; maximum unobstructed distance; exact slope/edge thresholds and behavior at abrupt drops; the stop cause in the 3-second sequence; and original kick behavior for pumped bombs. These require controlled input footage or instrumented original-game measurements rather than inference from multiplayer uploads.

## Implemented policy and diagnosis

The committed kick plants the player for four authority ticks (about 0.13 seconds), launches in the committed direction, then restores movement over five ticks (about 0.17 seconds). A wind-up is cancelled if its bomb disappears, is picked up, moves away, or the player falls/is interrupted. Timed fuses continue through the action; normal input edges are consumed rather than replayed on recovery. Continuous contact against a blocked bomb produces one action until the player separates or releases and re-engages. This deliberate short commitment answers the requested weight; the footage does not prove those exact input restrictions.

Ground speed remains 12 cells/second. The reproduced range defect was terrain rejection: a shot stopped at a downward terrace lip. Kicked bombs now follow gravity off those edges and retain horizontal motion after landing. Full-radius sweeps still reject uphill walls, pillars and corners, and ordinary throws still land at rest. Eleven integration cases cover long travel, repeated drops, connected ramps, lower-ground hits, walls/corners, water, fuse expiry and ordinary throws. Directed abrupt drops are an explicit adaptation to this game’s stepped map.

The smaller hit uses 2.2 horizontal and 2.6 upward speed. A regression with the previous impulse measured a 0.995-unit peak and failed the new bounded-stumble check. Our actual stock-temple recording measures a **0.301-unit peak and 1.10-unit horizontal displacement** before landing. The brief low hop, nearby prone pose, and recovery were inspected across frames from the actual game recording, not inferred only from authority tests. Grab/escape tests now start the carrier closer because the victim no longer travels toward the old distant fixture position.

The shoreline defect had a separate, reproduced cause: water cells carry tier metadata zero, but the rendered water plane is at −1 before flooding. Walking/falling used tier zero as the landing surface and could kill a player one height unit above the visible water. Player and falling-kick contact now use the actual water plane. Side/corner body-radius grazing remains safe while the center is supported on land; stepping off still falls and water/flood contact is still lethal. Tests preserve both safety and actual elimination.
