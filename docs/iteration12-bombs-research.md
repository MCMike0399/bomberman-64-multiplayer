# Iteration twelve: A placement, remote appearance and red power

Inspected 2026-10-20. The latest user clarification concerns **general A-button placement**, including an apparent ignored press with no bomb visible. It is not limited to running. This research supplies original-game references; diagnosing this project's placement rejection belongs to its implementation checks.

## Placement and departure

The [Nintendo-hosted original English manual](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/downloads/games_8/emanuals/nintendo_8/Manual_Nintendo64_Bomberman64_EN.pdf), printed pp. 4–5 / PDF page 4, assigns A to setting a bomb. While stationary, A once sets it and A again kicks it. Running into a planted bomb is the other kick route. The booklet does not specify a forward offset, a collision-test radius, a placement delay, or a rule that rejects an otherwise legal placement near an obstacle.

The [original Japanese action sheet](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/j/jackallsasaki/20220916/20220916214413.jpg), preserved in this [1997 manual scan](https://gamemanual.midnightmeattrain.com/entry/%E7%88%86%E3%83%9C%E3%83%B3%E3%83%90%E3%83%BC%E3%83%9E%E3%83%B3), separately illustrates A placement, foot contact/A twice for kicking, and R stopping. The placement illustration puts the bomb immediately in front of the standing character at foot level. This is instructional artwork, not a scale drawing that establishes exact placement geometry.

Direct footage was inspected at 0.05-second intervals in ignored `artifacts/research/iteration12/black-first-placement.jpg`: [XFactor1111 multiplayer, 00:03.30–00:04.35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPjnYY_Gomo&t=3s). Black moves left across the lower-right grass through approximately 03.30–03.40. A small dark bomb becomes visible immediately at/below the feet around 03.45–03.60. Black moves upward from it through 03.65–04.35 while the bomb remains stationary on the grass. This supports a near-foot placement and the ability to depart while leaving the bomb behind. It does not support automatically kicking the bomb on every departure. Inputs are hidden, so the footage does not establish whether black briefly stopped or changed stick direction at the instant of the A press, or whether the bomb's initial position was exactly underneath versus slightly ahead of the character.

An additional [Hawlo Green Garden sequence, 00:22.60–00:24.15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ZmKLNopSDQ&t=22s), sampled every 0.05 second in `placement-story-detail.jpg`, shows white maneuvering immediately beside pots, with a small dark planted object becoming visible beside the feet/pots as white moves away. Pots and the strong circular character shadow obscure the exact spawn instant. It is useful evidence of close placement beside obstacles, but unsuitable for deriving an exact placement offset or proving an uninterrupted moving-placement input sequence. The uploader identifies the recording as emulated.

A dependable implementation should therefore be judged on whether a legal A press visibly places a bomb near the feet, whether stationary A-again remains a distinct kick, and whether departure alone produces an unwanted kick. The original sources cannot explain this project's ignored-input symptom. Capacity, actual active bombs, obstruction geometry, cooldown/recovery state and the action selected for each press need direct project diagnostics.

## Blue remote bombs: primary visual evidence

The Japanese action sheet's top-right panel is explicitly titled **リモコン爆破** (remote detonation). It depicts a **blue spherical bomb with round green indicators** and a Z-button symbol. Its bomb-tower and bomb-jump illustrations use the same blue/green design. This is direct original publisher artwork identifying the remote appearance, stronger evidence than interpreting a capacity-pickup icon or a compressed multiplayer silhouette.

[Konami's official product page](https://www.konami.com/games/jp/ja/products/dl_bakubomberman_nintendo64_c/) identifies this as the game first released for Nintendo 64 in 1997. Its [official screenshot](https://www.konami.com/products_master/jp_publish/dl_bakubomberman_nintendo64_c/jp/ja/images/00001.jpg) shows the same blue shells with green indicators placed on steps and a platform. The screenshot alone does not label their mechanics, but corroborates the action sheet's blue/green visual design in actual game rendering. Both images were actually inspected and cached privately as `jp-action-sheet.jpg` and `konami-blue-bombs.jpg`; they are not new distributable game assets.

The English manual, printed pp. 26–27 / PDF page 15, states that the Remote pickup turns bombs into command-detonated bombs triggered by Z, and that the effect is lost on death or advancing to another stage. Its pink/purple heart-shaped pickup frame is a different visual object from the deployed blue remote bomb. Blue remote shells are supported by the original artwork, independently of our pickup design.

## Red power, blast size and thresholds

The English manual's Power entry says that firepower increases and objects that resist weaker bombs can be broken. Its Fire entry gives **regular bomb power up to level three and pumped bomb power up to level six**. Those are stated power levels, not geometric radius values, a red-specific radius multiplier, or a table of material thresholds. The [original Japanese item spread](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/j/jackallsasaki/20220916/20220916214355.jpg), also actually inspected (`jp-items.jpg`), likewise describes increased placed-bomb firepower and destruction of blocks a normal bomb cannot break. Neither inspected item spread supplies a controlled red-versus-normal radius ratio.

In [XFactor1111, 00:29.40–00:31.30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPjnYY_Gomo&t=29s), inspected at 0.1-second intervals in `red-blast-29-31.jpg`, a small red bomb is clearly visible on the lower-right grass through approximately 30.20. It becomes a cyan ring around 30.30–30.40 and a broad cyan/blue blast around 30.50–30.80. An ordinary orange blast is visible near the upper central pillar earlier in the sequence. This directly supports distinct red-shell/cyan-blast presentation and a substantial area effect. It is **not** a controlled equal-fire-level comparison: owner upgrades, pumping, perspective and blast phases differ. It cannot establish a numerical red radius advantage independently of those variables.

The user asked whether red bombs really have a larger blast; that question does **not** authorize inventing an extra radius boost. The inspected publisher evidence establishes increased firepower and destruction strength. It does not isolate a red-versus-normal geometric radius advantage, and the inspected footage is not a controlled comparison. Do not add or describe a red radius multiplier as an original-game fact on this evidence.

A follow-up search checked the official Konami product description, the English and Japanese item spreads, and the available [Bomberhackers decompilation repository](https://github.com/Bomberhackers/bm64). The official product description explains pumping, but supplies no red-versus-normal radius comparison. The repository is explicitly a work-in-progress decompilation and its visible game overlays are mostly jump-table/unknown-symbol material; no verified red-radius calculation was identified. Search results also surfaced fan guides describing huge red explosions, but those do not resolve an equal-fire, equal-pump comparison. The honest answer remains: **red's increased destruction power is established; a separate radius increase at otherwise identical settings is not established by this research**.

The user also explicitly requires remote and red modes to replace one another. Implement that requirement as an intentional project rule. The inspected publisher manual does not state mutual exclusivity. Search discovery found descriptions of combined red remote bombs in fan guides, but those secondary descriptions are not a basis for silently overriding the user's explicit rule or claiming that the original has exclusive modes.

## Implemented placement and diagnosis

The new action tries a .18-unit bias at the feet and falls back to the supported player center, with other players/bombs and stone still validated. The old distant .82-unit spawn could make its footprint touch a cell two grid steps from the player. The one-edge terrain connectivity check then rejected an empty flat spot. A regression reproduced the reported zero-active-bomb A failure before the change.

An owner can leave the initial overlap in any direction without accidentally kicking. Once clear, returning to the bomb uses ordinary collision/contact kick; a fresh second A beside it deliberately kicks. A placement cue and short hand motion mark accepted drops; capacity/space rejection now gets a visible cue. This implements the reference action without claiming an exact original offset or hitbox rule.

A second reproduced issue appeared in the full controller suite: the old generous lift range let a moving A hold retrieve the new near-foot bomb from behind. The next A would pump it instead of creating another bomb. Movement now cancels the fresh-bomb auto-lift candidate; holding the initial placement press while stationary still lifts normally. Keyboard and synthetic Nintendo/Xbox/PlayStation action paths are exercised separately from physical-device observation.

Red and remote are mutually exclusive inventory modes by explicit user request. No extra red radius multiplier was added. Blue shells with green indicators follow the original publisher artwork. Default bombs are 22% larger and pickups 20% larger; after the user's maximum-size correction, pump growth is additive .32 per press with target cap 2.3, preserving blast strength. Three pumps at base fire produce about 1.79× normal visual size.
